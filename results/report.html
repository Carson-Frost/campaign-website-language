How congressional candidates talk on their campaign websites, 2020–2026

Which words U.S. House and Senate general-election candidates used on their campaign websites, by party and election year. Each figure is the share of candidates whose site used a term at least once.

What changed

Shares are the percentage of each party's analyzed candidates whose campaign website used a term at least once. These findings are exploratory: they were picked after looking at all 57 terms, so they point to patterns worth testing rather than settled results. Every share in the term table below has a 95% range, and every Democratic minus Republican gap has a range and an adjusted p-value. Those ranges describe chance variation among the candidates analyzed, and they do not cover the candidates whose sites could not be analyzed. Select a finding to open its term below.

    Phrases that most separate the parties

    Words and phrases ranked by how strongly their use leans toward one party's candidates in that election. Each candidate counts once: the ranking compares how much of each candidate's text the word or phrase makes up, averaged over each party's candidates, by Welch's t-test (Lijffijt and others, 2016), so a phrase repeated on one site cannot top the list. No words are removed beforehand. Bars show the share of each party's candidates who used it.

    Coverage

    Candidates on each general-election ballot, how many had a known campaign website, and how many sites yielded at least 100 words of campaign text from the collection window.

    Checking 2026 against live sites

    The 2026 figures above use archived captures, like the other years. This table repeats them using a live crawl of every 2026 campaign site with a known address, taken in early October 2026. Live shares run a few points higher for nearly every term in both parties, because a live site exposes more pages and more text than an archive holds. That is why every year is compared on archived text only. The gaps between the parties are similar in both columns.
