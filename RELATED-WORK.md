# Related work

Earlier studies that collected or scored text from candidates' campaign websites, with how this study differs from each.

## Hilbig (2026)

U.S. House (2018–2024) and Senate (2002–2024) Candidate Websites

Archived text from Democratic and Republican candidate websites, House 2018 to 2024 and Senate 2002 to 2024, from the Wayback Machine, with measures of length, vocabulary and topic shares from a trained classifier.

How this study differs: this study counts specific words and adds 2026.

## Bailey and Reese (2026)

Ideological Moderation and Success in U.S. Elections, 2020–2022

Scored the ideology of House, Senate and governor candidates from their website and Twitter text in 2020 and 2022.

How this study differs: this study counts named words and shows how the shares changed, with ranges for candidates who could not be analyzed.

## Porter, Case and Treul (2025)

CampaignView, a Database of Policy Platforms and Biographical Narratives for Congressional Candidates

A database of policy platforms of House primary candidates in 2018, 2020 and 2022, collected by hand a week before each primary and labeled by topic.

How this study differs: this study covers general-election candidates, the House and the Senate, and counts words by code.

## Di Tella, Kotti, Le Pennec and Pons (2025)

Keep Your Enemies Closer: Strategic Platform Adjustments during US and French Elections

Used archived House candidate websites from 2002 to 2016 to show that candidates move toward the center between primary and general election.

How this study differs: this study reports shares of candidates using each term, not an ideology score.

## Druckman, Kifer and Parkin (2009)

Campaign Communications in U.S. Congressional Elections

Coded 736 candidate websites from 2002, 2004 and 2006 in the ten days before election day, and found that challengers and incumbents campaigned differently.

How this study differs: this study covers 2020 to 2026 and measures every site with the same code instead of coders.
